Interpretation. The belief in a burglary collapses from 0.284 to about 0.003. Nothing new was learned  
about the burglary directly. Rather, the earthquake fully explains why the alarm rang, so the alarm is no  
longer evidence for a burglary. This effect is called explaining away. Two causes of a common observed  
effect become negatively correlated once the effect is known, even though they are independent to  
begin with. Bayesian networks reproduce this reasoning pattern automatically; rule based systems  
usually cannot.  
  
  
5. Why bother with a Bayesian network?  
Every query above could in principle be answered from the full joint distribution table. So what does  
the network buy us?  
  
5.1 Far fewer numbers to specify  
Five Boolean variables have 2⁵ = 32 possible worlds. A full joint table needs 31 independent  
probabilities (the last is fixed because they sum to one). The network needs only the entries in its CPTs:  
  
  
        Node              Number of parents             Rows in CPT          Independent numbers  
        Burglary                    0                          1                      1  
        Earthquake                   0                          1                      1  
        Alarm                      2                          4                      4  
        JohnCalls                   1                          2                      2  
        MaryCalls                    1                          2                      2  
        Total                                                                       10  
  
  
Ten numbers instead of thirty one. This gap explodes as the model grows: with n Boolean variables the  
full table needs 2ⁿ − 1 entries, while a network in which each node has at most k parents needs at most  
n · 2ᵏ. For thirty variables with at most three parents each that is 240 numbers versus more than a  
billion. Every one of those numbers has to come from data or from an expert, so fewer numbers means  
a model that can actually be built.  
  
5.2 Local, meaningful parameters  
Each CPT entry answers a natural question that an expert or a dataset can address on its own, such as:  
how reliable is the alarm when there is a burglary? How often does Mary miss the alarm? The  
corresponding entries in the full joint table, such as the probability of ¬b, e, a, ¬j, m, are almost  
impossible to estimate directly.
